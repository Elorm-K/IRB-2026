## 2. Summary

This study investigates how instructors and teaching staff adopt, adapt, and sustain teaching approaches and tools related to professional skills and dispositions and generative AI use skills and dispositions. This includes learning and reflection involving social and individual reflection, including reflection groups and tools and approaches for learning and reflecting on generative AI use. For example, in a reflection group, a small number of students meet briefly (such as ~30 minutes) and regularly (such as weekly), with a guided agenda to scaffold reflection. Tools and approaches for learning and reflecting on generative AI use include, for example, the comparative video-supported "watch your replay" approach described in (Fernandez and Nelson 2026). For brevity, we will use "instructor" to refer to instructors and teaching staff.

The research team's prior work found that structured peer reflection groups in a course supported professional skills including iterative improvement, psychological safety, and communication, with effects students reported carrying beyond the course, and that a comparative reflection assignment on programming with and without generative AI supported learning about critical AI use (Fernandez & Nelson, 2026). Approaches like these only matter at scale if instructors beyond the original team can adopt them. Implementation research shows adoption depends on understanding adopters' contexts, barriers, and adaptations (Bauer & Kirchner, 2020; Shadle, Marker, & Earl, 2017), and prior research on reflection and peer support explains why the approaches themselves are expected to help students (Chan & Lee, 2021; Lim et al., 2022).

This is a mixed-methods study with qualitative and quantitative analysis. The components are:

- a) design workshops, including transcripts, notes and other artifacts created as part of the design workshops made with participants, including designs made by and adapted by participants
- b) recordings, transcripts, and/or notes from instructor-researcher consultation "office hours" sessions and interview sessions
- c) instructor surveys, including longitudinal surveys following-up on their experiences and adoption and adaptation more generally
- {{d) for students in collaborating instructors' courses, with student consent: analysis of reflection-group materials, pre- and post-course surveys, longitudinal follow-up surveys, and student and alumni interviews, to understand the effects of the adopted approaches on student outcomes}}

Design workshops with instructors, surveys and interviews about barriers and benefits, and consultation notes are collected and analyzed to understand what makes these approaches adoptable. Workshops may concern reflection groups, designing generative AI integration in courses, or designing reflection on generative AI use. The population of instructors for this study includes faculty, teaching staff such as lecturers and TAs, and other educators at the University of Maine and at other institutions. {{The study also includes students in collaborating instructors' courses where these approaches, or comparison surveys, are used.}}

For the purposes of this research, "adoption outcomes" refers to instructors' decisions to use, adapt, sustain, or not use these designs; the adaptations they make; the barriers and enablers they encounter; and their perceptions of the benefits and costs. {{"Student outcomes" refers to students' professional skills, reflection skills and habits, self-efficacy, sense of belonging and professional identity, and their experiences of these approaches.}}

### Research Questions

1. What barriers and enablers shape whether instructors and teaching staff adopt, adapt, and sustain teaching approaches and tools related to professional skills and dispositions and generative AI use skills and dispositions in their courses and institutions?

2. How do designs developed together with instructors and teaching staff differ across courses and institutional contexts, and what do those differences suggest about designing for adoption?

3. What adoption outcomes follow from participation in design workshops and consultations?

{{4. What are the effects of these approaches on student outcomes in courses beyond the research team's own, including at other institutions?}}

### Methods

This research is expected to run over five years under the NSF CAREER grant, with an initial round of instructor design workshops to start pending IRB approval in late Summer / early Fall 2026, offered roughly one to four times based on scheduling constraints, and offered on a semesterly/quarterly design cycle thereafter. Surveys for instructors will also be distributed, both to understand instructors more broadly, and even when not participating in a design workshop. Surveys will also help monitor change over time, including changes in participants that choose to participate in the design workshop(s). {{Classroom use in collaborating instructors' courses begins in a term following the first design workshops; we anticipate collaborating with roughly 10–20 courses per year. Consent precedes every activity below; see Section 5.}}

{{For instructor participants:}}

**1. Interest and barriers surveys.** Instructors are invited to an online confidential survey about their teaching context, their interest in these approaches, and the barriers and benefits they perceive. The survey will be administered via Qualtrics and will take approximately 10–15 minutes. The survey questions can be found in Appendix D.

**2. Design workshops.** Instructors are invited to design workshops which cover co-designing one or more of the following: (a) reflection-group designs, (b) the integration of generative AI into their courses, and (c) designs for reflection on generative AI use. In each workshop, instructors identify challenges and barriers to adoption and implementation in their own courses and institutions, and propose designs and improvements. Workshop screen recordings, transcripts, notes and the materials attendees produce are analyzed qualitatively. Workshops are held online via Zoom and typically run about one to two hours. Each workshop is recorded; however, webcam videos are deleted. Because workshops are group sessions, participants must agree to the recording in order to take part in a workshop. The workshop protocol is in Appendix B.

**3. Consultation ("office hours") sessions.** The PI holds by-appointment consultations for interested instructors on Zoom. For instructors who consent, the PI records and analyzes them as qualitative research on designing for adoption. Participants have a choice to agree to the recording or written notes are taken instead. With the instructor's permission, the research team may keep copies of teaching materials the instructor refers to or shares during a session; these are treated as consultation data and handled as described in Section 6. During these sessions instructors are told to not share any identifiable student data; if this happens by mistake, those will be deleted from any records made from the session.

**4. Optional interviews.** Consenting instructors take part in confidential interviews about their teaching context, perceived barriers and benefits, and their implementation experiences. Interviews take approximately 30–60 minutes, are conducted on Zoom or in person based on the participant's preference, and are audio-recorded; no video is recorded. The interview protocol is in Appendix E. Participants must agree to the recording, or written notes are taken instead. Interviews are covered by the instructor consent form in Appendix A.

**5. Longitudinal follow-up.** Consenting instructors receive invitations to take brief follow-up surveys (Appendix D) and/or follow-up interview for later semesters, about their adoption decisions, the adaptations they made, and their experiences. Follow-ups use the same procedures as the initial survey and interview, follow-up interviews use Appendix E.

{{**6. Faculty adaptation.** Instructors who adapt a design for their own course evaluation are encouraged to use the short pre- and post-course measures in Appendix J so different versions can be compared. Where an instructor wishes their students' data to be part of this research, their students are consented and take part under items 7–15 below.}}

{{For students in collaborating instructors' courses:}}

{{**7. Classroom use.** Collaborating instructors integrate reflection groups, reflection-on-AI-use activities, or both into their courses as a planned part of the course, following the team's materials as adapted with the instructor. The course activity occurs whether or not students consent to the research, and is not itself research.}}

{{**8. Group meetings and individual reflections.** Each group holds a roughly 30-minute meeting at a regular interval (for example, weekly), with an agenda designed by the instructor, for example: participants discuss what is going well in their learning and what is supporting it, give updates on their goal from the previous meeting, identify areas for potential improvement, and each choose one thing to improve before the next meeting. The reflection group meetings are not recorded; the research collects only what is written by consenting participants.}}

{{**9. Student consent.** Before any student data are collected, a member of the research team — never the host instructor — presents the study using the script in Appendix I and distributes the consent form (Appendix F). Consent is opt-in: a student takes part in the research only if they affirmatively indicate consent on the consent form, and no research data are collected about students who do not opt in. Participating in the research has no effect on assignments, activities, or grades, and the host instructor does not learn which students consented until after final grades are submitted.}}

{{**10. Student surveys.** Consenting students respond to pre- and post-course surveys with the student outcome measures (Appendix J). Surveys are confidential, are administered online via Qualtrics, and take approximately 10–15 minutes each. There is no research compensation for the pre- and post-course surveys.}}

{{**11. Student reflection materials.** With consent, students' reflection-group materials — group meeting notes and individual written reflections, including reflection assignments — are analyzed by the research team. No grades, academic records, or other student-records data are collected, and reflection group meetings are not recorded.}}

{{**12. Longitudinal follow-up.** Consenting students are invited to confidential follow-up surveys roughly every six months after the course, for up to four years (up to eight surveys). Follow-up surveys are administered online via Qualtrics, take approximately 10–15 minutes, and are covered by the course consent form (Appendix F); submitting a follow-up survey indicates continued consent. The survey content is in Appendix K, and the invitation text is in Appendix I. Students are offered a $10 electronic VISA gift card for each submitted follow-up survey (see Section 9).}}

{{**13. Student and alumni interviews.** A subset of consenting students and former students is invited to confidential interviews about their experiences, the professional skills they use, and changes over time. Interviews take approximately 30–60 minutes, are conducted on Zoom or in person based on the participant's preference, and are audio-recorded; if the participant chooses to share their screen (for example, to show an example of their work), the screen-share is also recorded; no webcam video is recorded. Participants must agree to the recording, or written notes are taken instead. Interviews are covered by the separate consent form in Appendix H, the interview protocol is in Appendix L, and the invitation text is in Appendix I. Participants are offered a $25 electronic VISA gift card per interview (see Section 9).}}

{{**14. Student design input and group remedies.** Consenting students may be invited to a design session held during a class session, to an end-of-semester retrospective about what should change, and to a brief optional mid-course feedback survey so the team can adjust during the term; these are conducted in class or online via Qualtrics, are not recorded, and are not compensated. Notes and materials from these are analyzed qualitatively. Groups do not always work well: a student in a group that has stopped functioning may join a different group, if that group agrees to add a member, or form a new group.}}

{{**15. Comparison courses.** Where a collaborating instructor's institution offers a similar course without these approaches, students in that course may be recruited for surveys only, as a comparison group, and are also invited to the longitudinal follow-up surveys. Because no course activity is involved, consent is obtained at the beginning of their initial survey using the consent form in Appendix G; submitting the survey indicates consent. The recruitment script is in Appendix I. Comparison-course surveys use the same instruments, platform, and time estimates as items 10 and 12, with the same compensation terms (no compensation for the pre- and post-course surveys; $10 per submitted follow-up survey).}}

## 6. Confidentiality

Initially, all study data will be stored on a secure cloud storage platform that encrypts all data at rest and in transit with at least AES-256 bit encryption and is password protected. Survey data will be collected online through Qualtrics. The survey responses are deleted from Qualtrics once they have been archived within 14 days as described below.

For any group activities such as the workshops {{and the reflection groups}}, as researchers we cannot guarantee confidentiality of responses but we will ask participants not to share others' responses outside the session.

{{For student data collected during a course, archiving occurs within 14 days after the end of the course; during archiving, any data from students who did not consent to the research are filtered out and deleted by looking up their email addresses and removing the records associated with them. Reflection group meetings are not recorded; the research collects only what consenting participants write. The host instructor does not learn which students consented until after final grades are submitted, and students' survey responses, reflection materials, and interview participation are never shared with the host instructor or teaching assistants in identifiable form.}}

Within 14 days of collection, all data will be archived to a separate archival cloud storage folder, with all participant identifiers (such as email addresses) being replaced with a randomly generated encrypted key. The key file that maps to participants' email addresses and participant demographic data will be stored in a separate cloud storage folder. After 5 years from participation, their email address will be deleted from the key file; for example, for the Fall 2026 semester, the key will be deleted by the end of December 2031. This is to allow participants to withdraw their consent to participating in the research later if they wish. We can only delete participant data until the key is deleted.

Resources from the workshop will be copied onto the archival secure cloud storage folder, with any participant identifiers replaced with their encrypted key. The archived data will be retained indefinitely. Survey data will be copied into the archival secure cloud storage folder and all participant identifiers replaced with their encrypted key. The archived data will be retained indefinitely.

Notes from the research and materials produced during the design workshops, consultation sessions, and interview sessions, and recordings and transcripts, will be copied onto the archival secure cloud storage folder, with any participant identifiers replaced with their encrypted key. This will include any screen recordings (which will not include participants' faces) and audio recordings from the design workshops, consultation sessions, and qualitative interviews, and transcriptions of the recordings. The archived data will be retained indefinitely, except for the original audio recordings which will be deleted at the same time as when participants' email addresses are deleted from the key mapping file. For example, for the Fall 2026 semester, the recordings will be deleted by the end of December 2031. The audio recordings are retained because we have found it a negative limitation during the analysis in other research projects when we could not go back to the actual recordings.

{{Student and alumni interview recordings, transcripts, and reflection materials are handled through the same archival, key-file, and retention process. Recordings are transcribed using a secure automated transcription service. Recordings made on a device for in-person interviews are removed from the device to the secure cloud storage platform within 72 hours.}}

{{Information needed to issue compensation is collected on a separate form (Appendix N), administered independently of the research instruments. It is stored apart from research data, is never joined to a participant's responses, and is used only to record the payment.}}

In summary, data from research participants will be retained indefinitely in de-identified form.

## 7. Risks to participants

There are risks of time and inconvenience to participants. There is a minimal risk of a data breach; we expect a potential risk of embarrassment in that event. This is mitigated by the de-identification, storage, and retention procedures described in Section 6.

{{For students, because their instructor collaborates with the research team, there could be perceived pressure to participate. This is mitigated by the protections described in Section 4: the course activity happens whether or not a student takes part in the research, consent is opt-in and administered by the research team rather than the instructor, the host instructor does not learn who participated until after final grades are submitted, and participation has no effect on grades.}}

{{In reflection groups, information a student shares could be repeated by other group members. Group members are asked to respect each other's confidentiality, but it cannot be guaranteed; participants are informed of this limitation in the consent process.}}

The surveys and interviews will make clear in the introduction that any question can be skipped if the participant does not feel comfortable answering.

## 8. Benefits

There are no direct benefits to participants, but participants may learn and reflect critically on their learning, professional skills, self-efficacy, and professional identity over time, which may help them develop skills useful in their education and careers, and they may benefit from community of practise, peer support, and professional networks formed through the groups.

The overall benefits of the research include improving understanding of scalable, adoptable, and effective reflection-based practices for supporting learning, professional skills, and lifelong learning across educational and professional settings. Findings may inform pedagogical practice, support adoption across institutions, and contribute to understanding how to sustain professional growth after graduation.

## Appendix B: Design workshop protocol and consultation procedure

**Design workshops**

Workshops are held online via Zoom facilitated by the research team, and typically run about one to two hours. A workshop may concern reflection-group designs, integrating generative AI into courses, designing reflection on generative AI use, or a combination.

Ground rules, read at the start of every workshop:

- Participation is voluntary, and you may leave at any time.
- So everyone feels safe to participate freely, please do not share others' contributions outside the workshop without asking for their permission.
- Attending this workshop does not obligate you to use any of these designs in your course.
- To help us learn as much as we can from your perspective and contributions, this workshop is being audio recorded and transcribed, and screen-shares are recorded (we will delete the webcam videos from the recording). Agreeing to the recording is part of taking part in the workshop. If you accidentally share something unintentionally or want anything you say or share removed from the recording, just let us know during or after the recording and we will delete it from the recording.

The facilitator opens by stating the purpose of the session, then attendees work through the topics they came for: what they have tried and how it went, what makes adoption hard in their context, and what designs or adaptations would work for their courses. The research team provides a brief starting point presentation including current materials and adaptations others have contributed. Beyond the ground rules and the session's stated purpose, attendees and the research team discuss and co-design how the session's time is used, with the research team member(s) acting as a facilitator.

**Consultation ("office hours") procedure**

At the start of each session:

> "Before we start: as we discussed in the consent form, I'd like to record our conversation, to help understand how instructors adopt these approaches and how to improve them. They're confidential and you can tell me during our discussion or afterwards, to leave something out — a part of this conversation or the whole session. Is that OK for today?"

After the participant answers, the member of the research team says "So, tell me about your situation and how I can help you today? Oh, and as normal please avoid sharing identifiable student data with me; if that happens by accident we will just delete that from the recording." then proceeds to engage in an unstructured conversation with the participant.

At the end of the session, the member of the research team may ask for copies of materials shared by the instructor. The instructor is not required to share them.

> "If you're comfortable, could we keep a copy of <describe the documents> you shared today, for research purposes?"
