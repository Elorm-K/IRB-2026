## Why merged rather than staged

The split rested on the student arm arriving later as a cheap modification. UMaine's rules say otherwise: a modification changing **two or more** of {population, procedures, purpose} *"becomes a new study."* The student arm changes two. Staging therefore costs two full applications. Nothing in the rules requires per-population protocols, the instructions contemplate multi-population studies, and the board has already approved reflection groups in courses under the team's course protocol.

### Not ported, deliberately

- **"Shared affinity" as a matching criterion.** IRB #1 collects hobbies and interests at intake, so
  it can match on affinity. IRB #2's matching survey (Appendix L) collects availability plus optional
  gender and disability status only. Naming a data type nothing collects would create a fresh
  inconsistency, so only the loosening was taken, not the new criterion.
- **First-survey-only consent.** IRB #1 now shows its survey consent form at the first survey only.

- **Genericizing storage to "a cloud data platform."** IRB #1's final wording does this, but **both
  approved protocols name Google Drive explicitly** (75 and 29 occurrences). IRB #2's Google Drive

- **`<insert link>` placeholders.** IRB #1 uses them; IRB #2 uses `____`. Angle brackets are silently
  stripped by a Google Docs markdown upload, so `____` is the safer form here.
- **Dropping the non-research education activities from §2.** IRB #1 deleted its "high-school visitors
  are not research participants" sentence, which was pure pre-emption. IRB #2's sentence delimits
  *named grant activities* and carries the coverage story, so it stays.
