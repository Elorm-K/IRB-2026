### Section 2 — additional methods items

7. Deployment. Collaborating instructors integrate reflection groups into their courses as a planned part of the course, following the team's materials as adapted with the instructor: a matching survey (Appendix J); small groups, normally of 3–5 members, matched on shared gender, shared disability status, and overlapping availability, none of which is disclosed to other students; an icebreaker activity; regular short group meetings with a guided agenda; and brief individual written reflections (Appendix K). Topic modules may be used, including modules on critical generative AI use. The course activity occurs regardless of whether students consent to the research.

8. Student consent. Before any student data are collected, students receive an electronic consent form (Appendix F) and a recruitment script is read in class by a member of the research team (Appendix G). Participation in the research has no effect on assignments, activities, or grades. The host instructor does not administer consent, does not see who consented, and will not know who participated in the research until after final grades are submitted. Students opt out by emailing a member of the research team, who is never the instructor of their course.

9. Student surveys. Consenting students complete pre- and post-course surveys of approximately 10–15 minutes each with the study's student outcome measures (Appendix H), and are invited to longitudinal follow-up surveys roughly every 6 months after the course, for up to 4 years (up to 8 surveys total), with consent indicated by submission of each survey (Appendix I).

10. Student data. With consent, students' reflection-group materials — agenda notes and individual written reflections, including responses to any topic modules — are analyzed by the research team. No grades, academic records, or other student-records data are collected.

11. Group remedies. Groups do not always work well. A student in a group that has stopped functioning may join a different group, which opts in to adding a member, or form a new group with other students. Where this happens, the research team may also invite the affected students to help design a better response for future courses; notes from those conversations are analyzed qualitatively. Attendance at group meetings is not graded, so a student who does not find the group useful may simply stop attending without academic consequence.

12. Student design input. Students in collaborating courses may be invited to a design session held during a class session, and to an end-of-semester retrospective in which they identify what they improved, how other members helped, and what should change about the design. Notes and materials from these are analyzed qualitatively. Students may also be invited to a brief optional mid-course feedback survey so the team can adjust the design during the semester.

13. Student and alumni interviews. A purposeful subset of students and former students is invited to confidential interviews about their experiences, their professional-skill behaviors, changes over time, and — for those who have graduated — how these carry into work. Interviews take approximately 30–60 minutes, are conducted online or in person, and are audio-recorded; no video is recorded. Participants may be asked to describe specific instances and, if they wish, to show relevant work products; nothing is retained from a work product beyond the researcher's notes unless the participant separately agrees.

14. Comparison courses. Where a collaborating instructor's institution — including the University of Maine — offers a similar course without reflection groups, students in that course may be recruited for surveys only, as a comparison group, and are also invited to the longitudinal follow-up surveys. Because no course activity is involved, consent is obtained through a consent statement at the beginning of each survey, with submission indicating consent, using the comparison-course adaptation in Appendix I.

15. External sites. Before any activity in a course at another institution, the team obtains site approval — a letter of support from the appropriate official at that institution, requested per Appendix L — and completes whatever human-subjects review arrangement that institution requires, which may be a reliance agreement with the University of Maine, an exempt or not-research determination, or local review by that institution's IRB. Documentation of the arrangement is provided to the UMaine IRB before any research activity begins at that site.

An instructor may withdraw their course from the study at any time. Data already collected from consenting students are retained per Section 6, but no new data are collected from that course.

### Section 6, 7, 9 — additions

Section 6: No student grades, academic records, or other education-records data are collected under this protocol; classroom recordings are not made under this protocol.

Section 6: If a student previously participated in related reflection-group research led by this research team and consented to the research use of their data in that study, the team may link that data with their data from this study to help understand their experience and outcomes over time. These related studies address the same research questions and use the same student outcome measures, and any linked data receive the same protections described in this section.

Section 7: For students, because their instructor collaborates with the research team, there could be perceived pressure to participate. This is mitigated by the deployment protections in Section 4. Because reflection groups involve sharing among peers, personal information shared in a group could be disclosed by another member; students are asked to respect confidentiality and are informed that it cannot be guaranteed.

Section 9: Host courses are encouraged to grant a small amount of course credit for completing the pre- and post-course surveys; where granted, credit is for submitting the survey, not for the content of answers, and the survey is available to every student in the course regardless of research participation. For each longitudinal follow-up survey a student submits, the student is offered a $10 electronic VISA gift card, sent by email within 48 hours of submission; students must reach the end of the survey to receive compensation, though they may skip individual questions. Pre- and post-course surveys are not compensated. Students who complete an interview are offered a $25 electronic VISA gift card, sent by email within 48 hours.
