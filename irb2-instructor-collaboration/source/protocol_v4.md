## 2. Summary

This study investigates how instructors adopt, adapt, and sustain two related teaching approaches: structured peer reflection groups, and reflection on students' use of generative AI. The research team's prior work showed that structured peer reflection groups in courses support professional skills such as iterative improvement, psychological safety, and communication (Nelson et al., 2025), and that a comparative reflection assignment on programming with and without generative AI supported learning about critical AI use (Fernandez & Nelson, 2025). Approaches like these only achieve broad impact if instructors beyond the original team can adopt them, and implementation research shows that adoption depends on understanding adopters' contexts, barriers, and adaptations (Bauer & Kirchner, 2020; Shadle, Marker, & Earl, 2017).

Following the funded CAREER project's plan, this protocol therefore treats design itself as research. Design workshops with instructors, surveys and interviews about barriers and benefits, and consultation notes are collected and analyzed to understand what makes these approaches adoptable. Workshops may concern reflection groups, designing generative AI integration in courses, or designing reflection around generative AI use.

The participants in this study are instructors, faculty, and other educators at the University of Maine and at other institutions. This study does not involve their students. Research with students in collaborating instructors' courses is not part of this protocol; if the team offers that in the future, it will be submitted as a modification to this protocol or as a separate protocol before any such activity begins.

This protocol is separate from, and complementary to, two other protocols in the same program: the team's approved course-based protocol ("Metacognitive Reflection Groups for Improving Student Learning Outcomes in Computing and Programming Courses and Other Courses"), which covers reflection groups in the team's own courses, and the team's protocol for reflection groups outside of classes, submitted separately. The education and outreach activities described in the funded project — a community group for students working on enabling skills, hackathons and community events, and a high-school outreach program involving minors — are not research activities, and no data are collected from them under this protocol.

Because the research team develops and advocates the designs being adopted, this protocol separates collaboration from research participation at every step. The three separable choices — working with the team, using a design in a course, and allowing research use of data — and the protections attached to each are set out in Section 4 under Coercion considerations.

For the purposes of this research, "adoption outcomes" refers to: instructors' decisions to use, adapt, sustain, or not use these designs; the adaptations they make; the barriers and enablers they encounter; and their perceptions of the benefits and costs for themselves and their students. The term "adoption outcomes" is used throughout rather than re-listing its contents.

Research Questions:

1. What barriers and enablers shape whether instructors adopt, adapt, and sustain reflection-group pedagogies and reflection on generative AI use in their courses and institutions?
2. How do designs developed together with instructors differ across courses and institutional contexts, and what do those differences suggest about designing for adoption?
3. What adoption outcomes follow from participation in design workshops and consultations?

Methods:

This research is expected to run over multiple years under the NSF CAREER grant, with an initial round of instructor design workshops anticipated in fall 2026 and a yearly faculty adoption design cycle thereafter. Instructors take part in the following activities. Consent precedes any of them; see Section 5.

1. Interest and barriers survey. Instructors are invited to a survey about their teaching context, their interest in reflection-group and reflection-on-AI-use pedagogies, and the barriers and benefits they perceive, with an option to volunteer for a follow-up interview (Appendix C). The survey takes approximately 10–15 minutes and is administered through a secure survey platform.

2. Optional interviews. Consenting instructors take part in confidential interviews about their teaching context, perceived barriers and benefits, and — for those who tried the designs — their implementation experiences. Interviews take approximately 30–60 minutes, are conducted online or in person, and are audio-recorded; no video is recorded. If an instructor prefers not to be recorded, written notes are taken instead. The interview guide is in Appendix E and the interview consent form is Appendix B.

3. Design workshops. Instructors are invited to design workshops to shape (a) reflection-group designs, (b) the integration of generative AI into their courses, and/or (c) designs for reflection on generative AI use — including for their own courses and institutions, identifying challenges and barriers to adoption, and proposing designs and improvements. Workshops invite instructors who integrated a design, attempted to and encountered barriers, or chose not to integrate. Ground rules read at the start state that critiques are confidential and are reported only in de-identified form. Workshop notes and the materials attendees produce are analyzed qualitatively; a workshop is recorded only if everyone present agrees, and anyone may ask that recording be paused (Appendix F). Workshops are held online and typically run about one to two hours. An initial round is anticipated in fall 2026, repeated each design cycle, and also including roughly yearly adoption-cycle workshops.

4. Consultation ("office hours") notes. The PI holds by-appointment consultations for interested instructors. For instructors who consent, the PI takes notes and analyzes them as qualitative research on designing for adoption. Consultations are not recorded; notes may be handwritten and are transcribed and stored under the procedures in Section 6. Note-taking for research is confirmed verbally at the start of each session, and an instructor may ask that any part of a session, or a whole session, be left out of the research record — at the time or later. Instructors who do not consent receive the same consultation support.

5. Longitudinal follow-up. Consenting instructors receive brief follow-up surveys (Appendix D) and/or invitations to a follow-up interview in later semesters, about their adoption decisions, the adaptations they made, and their experiences.

6. Faculty adaptation. Instructors who adapt a design for their own course evaluation are encouraged to use a short set of pre- and post-course outcome measures, so that different versions can be compared. Under this protocol, the research team collects only the instructor's own account of that evaluation. Collecting data from their students would require the modification or separate protocol described above.

Instructors may withdraw from the research at any time while continuing to use the materials and receive the team's support. Data already collected are retained per Section 6, and no new data are collected from that instructor.

Data collected:

1.1 Instructor survey responses: teaching context, interest, perceived barriers and benefits, and email address; and instructor follow-up survey responses in later semesters (Appendices C and D).

1.2 Instructor interview recordings and transcripts, or written notes where an instructor prefers not to be recorded (guide in Appendix E).

1.3 Design workshop notes, the materials attendees produce, and recordings where everyone present agreed (Appendix F).

1.4 Consultation session notes for consenting instructors only, including transcriptions of handwritten notes. There is no separate instrument; notes follow the design and adoption topics in Appendix F.

No student data of any kind are collected under this protocol.

## 4. Participant recruitment

Participant Population:

Instructors, faculty, lecturers, and other educators at the University of Maine and at other institutions who teach, or are interested in teaching with, reflection-group pedagogies or reflection on generative AI use — including instructors who attempted integration and encountered barriers, and instructors who chose not to integrate. All participants are age 18 or older; individuals younger than 18 are excluded. Over the protocol's duration, the team expects approximately 50–100 instructors to participate.

Participant Identification and Recruitment Procedures:

Instructors are identified and invited through the College of Computer, Cyber, and Software Engineering mailing list and other institutional mailing lists, targeted email invitations, professional networks and conferences, and existing collaborators (letters of collaboration accompany the funded grant). Once the designs have positive results to report, the team will also recruit more broadly through public announcements, a project website, and social media. Recruitment texts are in Appendix G.

Recruitment messages separate the offers explicitly: instructors may work with the team — adopting materials, attending workshops, receiving support — and may separately choose whether to be research participants. Either is possible without the other.

Coercion considerations: the research team holds no supervisory authority over participating instructors, and does not evaluate them, employ them, or make decisions about their advancement. However, because collaboration may carry perceived professional stakes, and because the research team develops the designs instructors are asked to evaluate, the protocol adds the following protections. Research consent is separated from collaboration, as above. Attending a workshop does not obligate an instructor to use any design in their course, and an instructor may use the materials without their course or their students taking part in research. Individual adoption decisions — including declining — are confidential and reported only in de-identified form. An instructor may withdraw from the research at any time while continuing to use the materials and receive support, and withdrawing is not disclosed.

If a member of the research team is also a colleague of a participating instructor in a supervisory or evaluative relationship, that team member does not recruit, consent, interview, or analyze identifiable data for that instructor.

## 5. Informed consent

Informed consent is obtained electronically, and consent precedes any research activity.

There are two consent forms. All instructors review and electronically sign the consent form for participation (Appendix A) before taking part in any research activity, including before completing the interest and barriers survey and before attending a workshop from which data are analyzed. Instructors who take part in an interview review and sign a separate interview consent form (Appendix B) before the interview begins, because an interview is a distinct activity with its own recording and compensation terms. No separate consent form is used for the surveys, which are covered by Appendix A.

The protocol does not fix a single mechanism for administering Appendix A. It may be sent by email, presented at the start of an information session or a workshop invitation, or presented immediately before the survey. What is fixed is that consent occurs before any data are collected for research.

For consultation sessions, note-taking for research is additionally confirmed verbally at the start of each session, and the instructor may ask that any part of a session, or the whole session, be left out of the research record at the time or later.

For design workshops, recording occurs only if everyone present agrees; permission is requested at the start of each workshop, and anyone may ask that recording be paused.

## 6. Confidentiality

Participants in design workshops will be asked to respect the confidentiality of other attendees and not share what others contribute outside the workshop. However, we cannot guarantee complete confidentiality in a group setting. Participants are informed of this limitation in the consent process.

Individual instructors' adoption decisions, workshop contributions, and consultation content are confidential: they are reported only in de-identified form, and are not shared with the instructor's department, institution, or colleagues.

Initially, all data will be stored in a secure cloud platform that encrypts all data at rest and in transit, is password protected, and is accessible only to the research personnel named in Section 3 and to additional trained personnel added under the PI's supervision. Survey data are collected and stored in a secure survey platform with the same protections.

Within 14 days of collection, all data will be archived to a separate archival folder on the secure cloud platform, with all participant identifiers (such as email addresses) replaced with a randomly generated encrypted key. Handwritten consultation notes are transcribed and archived the same way. During this archiving, all data from individuals not consenting to the research will be filtered out and deleted, by looking up their email addresses and removing the associated records. The key file that maps to participants' email addresses and participant demographic data will be stored in a separate folder. Five years after a participant's participation ends, their email address will be deleted from the key file; for example, for an instructor whose participation ends in Fall 2026, the key entry will be deleted by the end of December 2031. This is to allow participants to withdraw their consent later if they wish. We can only delete participant data while the key exists.

De-identified quotations from survey responses, interview transcripts, workshop notes, and consultation notes may be used in publications and presentations, with no participant, course, or institution identified. De-identified data may be shared with collaborating researchers outside the research team for analysis, and may be deposited in a public research repository to meet the funder's public-access requirements. Original recordings are never shared or published.

The archived, de-identified data will be retained indefinitely, except for original interview and workshop recordings, which will be deleted at the same time as when a participant's email address is deleted from the key file, five years after their participation ends. For example, for a participant whose participation ends in Fall 2026, the recordings will be deleted by the end of December 2031.

In summary, the following data from participants who take part in the research will be retained indefinitely in de-identified form: instructor survey and follow-up survey responses (1.1), instructor interview transcripts and notes (1.2), design workshop notes and produced materials (1.3), and consultation session notes (1.4). Original interview and workshop recordings are deleted as described above.

Participants' privacy and right to withdraw from the study will be respected, so we will delete any of their data upon request as long as the request is within 5 years of the end of their participation, as otherwise their email address will have been deleted from the key file.

## 7. Risks to participants

There are risks of time and inconvenience to participants. There is a minimal risk of a data breach; we expect a potential risk of embarrassment in that event. This is mitigated by the de-identification, storage, and retention procedures described in Section 6.

For instructors, there is a potential professional-reputation risk if their adoption struggles, their critical opinions, or their decision not to adopt became known. This is mitigated by the confidentiality protections in Section 6 — de-identified reporting, and no sharing with institutions, departments, or colleagues — by the workshop ground rules, and by the separation of collaboration from research participation described in Section 4.

Because the research team develops and advocates the designs being evaluated, instructors may feel some pressure to report favorably or to adopt. This is mitigated by the protections in Section 4: consent separated from collaboration, no obligation to adopt following a workshop, confidential adoption decisions, and withdrawal at any time without disclosure and without losing support.

In workshop settings, information shared with other instructors could be disclosed by them; participants are asked to respect confidentiality and are informed that it cannot be guaranteed.

The surveys make clear in the introduction that any question can be skipped if the participant does not feel comfortable answering.

## 8. Benefits

There are no direct benefits guaranteed to participants. Instructors may benefit from course materials developed together with other instructors, from implementation support, and from a community of practice around reflection-group pedagogy and reflection on generative AI use. Instructors may find the workshops and consultations useful for their teaching whether or not they adopt any of the team's designs. Participation may also give instructors a clearer account of their own barriers and priorities, which prior research on reflection suggests is itself useful (Chan & Lee, 2021; Lim et al., 2022).

The overall benefits of the research include understanding how evidence-based pedagogies for professional skills and for critical and reflective generative AI use can be adopted and sustained across courses and institutions. By developing scalable, adoptable designs and open materials, the research aims to improve computing students' professional readiness and to support adoption across rural and urban computing programs.

## 9. Compensation

Instructors who take part in an interview are offered a one-time $25 electronic VISA gift card, sent by email within 48 hours of the interview. The $25 is paid once for taking part in an interview, not once per interview: an instructor who is interviewed again in a later semester receives no further payment. Instructors must reach the end of an interview to receive the compensation, though they may decline to answer any question; an instructor who stops partway through does not receive the gift card. Because the total compensation any instructor can receive under this protocol is $25, no participant will reach the $75 threshold that triggers University tax reporting.

No payments are made for the interest and barriers survey, the follow-up surveys, design workshops, or consultations.

## Appendix E: Instructor interview guide

Semi-structured. Questions follow the topics in the funded plan and are informed by implementation-research interview guides based on the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (Damschroder et al., 2009) and by studies of faculty drivers and barriers in undergraduate STEM education reform (Shadle, Marker, & Earl, 2017). Interviewers follow up on what the participant raises rather than adhering to the order below.

1. Teaching context: Tell me about the course or courses you teach — your students, class size, format, and what a typical week looks like.

2. Perceived benefits: What benefits, if any, do you see in reflection groups for your students? What about in having students reflect on their use of generative AI?

3. Perceived barriers: What barriers or concerns would you face, or did you face, in integrating these approaches? Which matter most to you?

4. Integration experiences, for those who tried: Walk me through how you integrated the approach into your course. What worked, and what was hard?

5. Deciding not to integrate, where applicable: What led to your decision not to integrate, or to stop? What, if anything, would change that decision?

6. Adaptations: What changes did you make, or would you need to make, to the design for your course, and why?

7. Generative AI in your context: How are you and your department currently handling generative AI in courses? What would make reflection on AI use workable for you?

8. Institutional factors: How do your department and institution view teaching innovation? What incentives, constraints, or expectations shape your decisions about adopting new teaching practices?

9. Support for adoption: What support, materials, or evidence would enable you to adopt or sustain these approaches?

10. Closing: Is there anything else about your context or experiences we should understand?

## Appendix F: Design workshop protocol and consultation procedure

### Design workshops

Workshops are held online, facilitated by the research team, and typically run about one to two hours. Attendees include instructors who integrated a design, attempted to and encountered barriers, or chose not to integrate. A workshop may concern reflection-group designs, integrating generative AI into courses, designing reflection on generative AI use, or a combination.

Ground rules, read at the start of every workshop:

- Participation is voluntary, and you may leave at any time.
- What is shared here is confidential — please do not share others' contributions outside the workshop.
- The research team reports findings only in de-identified form, and does not share your contributions or your adoption decisions with your institution, department, or colleagues.
- Attending this workshop does not obligate you to use any of these designs in your course.
- Recording occurs only if everyone present agrees, and anyone may ask that recording be paused at any point.

The facilitator opens by stating the purpose of the session, then attendees work through the topics they came for: what they have tried and how it went, what makes adoption hard in their context, and what designs or adaptations would work for their courses. The team provides its current materials as a starting point. Beyond the ground rules and the session's stated purpose, attendees direct how the session's time is used, and the facilitator follows the group.

Data collected: facilitator and attendee notes, the materials attendees produce during the session, and recordings only where everyone present agreed.

### Consultation ("office hours") procedure

Consultations are by appointment, are not recorded, and last as long as the instructor finds useful. The PI takes notes, which may be handwritten and are transcribed and stored under the procedures in Section 6.

Verbal confirmation, read at the start of each session with a consenting instructor:

> "Before we start: as we discussed in the consent form, I'd like to take notes on our conversation for our research on how instructors adopt these approaches. I'm not recording — these are just my notes. They're confidential and reported only in de-identified form. You can tell me at any time, today or later, to leave something out — a part of this conversation or the whole session. Is that OK for today?"

Instructors who have not consented to the research receive the same consultation support, and no notes are taken for research.
